The Critical Threshold

The model highlights a critical compensation threshold:

θ* = h/(h+m) ≈ 20%
  • Below θ*: Extraction dominates → creator decline
  • Above θ*: Mutualism dominates → sustainable growth

Where: h = extraction intensity, m = mutualism potential, θ = compensation rate

1. Lotka-Volterra Core Dynamics

The ecosystem evolves according to coupled differential equations, similar to predator-prey models but with potential for mutualism.

Creator Dynamics (dC/dt)

$$\frac{dC}{dt} = \underbrace{rC\left(1 - \frac{C}{K}\right)}_{\text{logistic growth}} - \underbrace{h(1-\theta)AC}_{\text{extraction harm}} + \underbrace{m\theta AC}_{\text{mutualism benefit}} - \underbrace{\alpha\phi_w AC(1-\theta)}_{\text{traffic diversion}}$$
Interpretation: Creators grow logistically, but face pressure from AI extraction (reduced by compensation θ) and traffic diversion to AI answers. Mutualism benefits (AI tools, discovery) scale with compensation.
r = 0.08 (growth rate) K = 1.0 (carrying capacity) h = extraction intensity θ = compensation rate m = mutualism coefficient α = traffic diversion rate

AI Platform Dynamics (dA/dt)

$$\frac{dA}{dt} = \underbrace{\beta CA(1 + \gamma Q)}_{\text{value from content}} - \underbrace{\delta A^2}_{\text{competition}} + \underbrace{I}_{\text{investment}}$$
Interpretation: AI platforms grow by extracting value from creator content (quality-adjusted). Face quadratic competition/depreciation. External investment provides baseline growth independent of content.
β = 0.35 (value extraction) γ = 0.40 (quality premium) δ = 0.15 (depreciation) I = investment rate

Web Traffic Dynamics (dW/dt)

$$\frac{dW}{dt} = \underbrace{-\alpha AW}_{\text{AI diversion}} + \underbrace{0.1(1-Q)(1-W)}_{\text{quality-driven recovery}}$$
Interpretation: Traffic to original content declines as AI platforms grow (AI answers replace clicks). Recovers somewhat if AI quality degrades (users return to sources).
α = traffic diversion rate W = fraction clicking through to sources

Content Quality Dynamics (dQ/dt)

$$\frac{dQ}{dt} = \underbrace{\epsilon(C - C^*)}_{\text{creator mass effect}} - \underbrace{\lambda(1-\theta)A}_{\text{exploitation degradation}} + \underbrace{\eta(\pi - \bar{\pi})}_{\text{revenue-quality link}}$$
Interpretation: Quality requires critical mass of creators (C*). Degrades when exploitation is high. Revenue above threshold enables investment in quality.
ε = 0.12 (quality response) C* = 0.35 (critical mass) λ = 0.10 (degradation rate)

2. Mechanism Design Constraints

The dynamics are modified by incentive compatibility constraints that trigger regime switches.

Individual Rationality (IR)

$$\pi_c = \underbrace{\theta \cdot p \cdot A}_{\text{AI licensing}} + \underbrace{p_{ad} \cdot W}_{\text{ad revenue}} + \underbrace{p_{sub} \cdot Q}_{\text{subscriptions}} - c \geq \bar{u}$$
Participation constraint: Creators participate only if total revenue (AI licensing + ads + subscriptions) minus costs exceeds their outside option (alternative employment).
If violated: Triggers EXIT REGIME — creator decline accelerates by factor of 3×

Incentive Compatibility (IC)

$$\theta \cdot p \cdot A \cdot \gamma \geq \Delta c = c_{high} - c_{low}$$
Quality incentive: Creators produce high-quality content only if the premium from quality (better AI licensing terms, more subscribers) exceeds the additional cost.
If violated: Triggers LOW-QUALITY REGIME — quality degrades 2× faster, "content farm" dynamics

3. Critical Thresholds & Regime Switching

Net Interaction Effect (Φ)

$$\Phi = m\theta - h(1-\theta) - \alpha\phi_w(1-\theta)$$
Key indicator: When Φ > 0, the ecosystem is mutualistic (creators benefit from AI). When Φ < 0, it's extractive (AI harms creators).

Critical Compensation Threshold

$$\theta^* = \frac{h + \alpha\phi_w}{h + \alpha\phi_w + m}$$
Policy target: Compensation must exceed θ* for sustainable coexistence. With current calibration: θ* ≈ 79%, far above status quo (~2%).

Regime Switching Rules

$$\text{Regime} = \begin{cases} \text{GROWTH} & \text{if IR satisfied and IC satisfied} \\ \text{EXIT} & \text{if IR violated (}\pi_c < \bar{u}\text{)} \\ \text{LOW-Q} & \text{if IC violated (quality unprofitable)} \end{cases}$$
The model switches between regimes based on whether incentive constraints are satisfied, creating non-linear dynamics and potential tipping points.

Modified Dynamics Under Regimes

$$\frac{dC}{dt}\bigg|_{\text{exit}} = \frac{dC}{dt}\bigg|_{\text{base}} - \kappa_1 \cdot C \cdot \mathbb{1}[\pi_c < \bar{u}]$$
$$\frac{dQ}{dt}\bigg|_{\text{low-Q}} = \frac{dQ}{dt}\bigg|_{\text{base}} - \kappa_2 \cdot Q \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{IC violated}]$$
Additional decay terms activate when constraints are violated, accelerating decline toward low-level equilibria.

4. Equilibrium Structure

Possible Equilibria

The system can settle into one of several equilibria depending on parameters:
  • Sustainable Coexistence: C*, A*, Q* all positive; IR and IC satisfied
  • Low-Quality Trap: Creators exist but produce low-quality content
  • Creator Extinction: C → 0; AI runs on legacy/synthetic content
  • Collapse: Both C and Q collapse; AI faces "data drought"

Stability via Jacobian

$$J = \begin{pmatrix} \partial \dot{C}/\partial C & \partial \dot{C}/\partial A \\ \partial \dot{A}/\partial C & \partial \dot{A}/\partial A \end{pmatrix}$$
Equilibrium stability determined by eigenvalues of Jacobian. Stable if Tr(J) < 0 and det(J) > 0.

Incentive Compatibility Analysis

Individual Rationality (IR) Constraint

$$\pi_c = \theta \cdot p \cdot A + w_0 - c(q) \geq \bar{u}$$
Creators participate iff expected profit (compensation + alternative income - cost) exceeds reservation utility.

Incentive Compatibility (IC) Constraint

$$\theta \cdot p \cdot A \cdot \gamma \geq \Delta c = c(q_H) - c(q_L)$$
High quality is incentive compatible iff the premium for quality exceeds the additional production cost.

Critical Compensation Threshold

$$\theta^{IC} = \frac{\Delta c}{p \cdot A \cdot \gamma}$$
Below this threshold, creators rationally produce low quality. As A increases, threshold drops (scale helps).

Mechanism Design Objective

$$\max_{\theta, \text{rules}} W = \alpha \cdot CS + (1-\alpha) \cdot \Pi_A + \beta \cdot \Pi_C$$
Social welfare trades off consumer surplus (CS), platform profit (Π_A), and creator welfare (Π_C).
